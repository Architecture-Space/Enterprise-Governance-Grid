# Enterprise Governance Grid

**A meaning backbone for multi-NATCO telecom: federated catalogs, certified Marketplace products, and AI that can trust what “Customer” means.**

## Abstract

Enterprises that operate across national companies (NATCOs) already own catalogs, data products, and AI tooling — yet they still disagree on what core words mean. *Kunde*, *Kupac*, *Ügyfél*, *Klient*, and *Customer* appear in Collibra, reports, Marketplace listings, and copilots as if they were the same thing. Often they are not.

The **Enterprise Governance Grid** separates **enterprise meaning** from catalog prose and product lifecycle. Meaning lives in a Git-backed **Semantic Control Plane** (namespaces, certified concepts with stable URIs, mapping records, federation edges). Business and Technical catalogs keep inventory and glossary ownership locally. Marketplace products **implement** certified concepts. Neo4j materializes contracts for traversal and impact analysis; it is not the system of record.

This white paper describes the problem, the four-pack contract architecture, the Customer 360 worked example across Global + DE / AT / HR / HU / PL, the runnable POC (UI + KG API + Neo4j), and the locked strategic recommendations (SQ1–SQ12), including the preferred semantic option for multi-tool Germany landscapes.

## 1. The multi-NATCO meaning crisis

### 1.1 Same word, different meaning

Telecom groups that federate national companies inherit parallel glossaries, CRM schemas, billing models, and product names. Labels converge in English decks and diverge in systems:

| Namespace | Local label | Risk if “aligned by string” |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `global` | Customer | SID-seeded enterprise concept |
| `natco-de` / `natco-at` | Kunde | Party vs account vs billing holder |
| `natco-hr` | Kupac | Contractual vs interacting party |
| `natco-hu` | Ügyfél | CRM party vs service subscriber |
| `natco-pl` | Klient | Retail vs wholesale scope |

Without an explicit crosswalk, every integration, KPI, and agent prompt becomes a negotiation.

### 1.2 Three failure modes

1. **Catalogs alone** — Collibra / Dataplex excel at inventory and glossary *prose*. They do not, by themselves, certify that a Marketplace product, a table column, and a NATCO term converge on one enterprise concept URI.
2. **Products without certified meaning** — Marketplace can publish “Customer 360” without proving it implements the enterprise Customer concept (and its identifier properties).
3. **AI without trusted context** — Copilots and agents invent meaning from table names and tribal knowledge when no traversable, approved semantic graph exists.

### 1.3 What “done” looks like for meaning

An analyst, a product consumer, or an agent should answer in one hop:

> Which Marketplace products **implement** the enterprise Customer concept, and which NATCO terms **federate** or **map** to it?

That is a governance-grid question, not a glossary search.

## 2. Thesis: meaning as a grid, not a glossary silo

### 2.1 One sentence

**Centrally governed meaning, federated catalog ownership, Marketplace enrichment, and open interchange — without asking NATCOs to give up their catalogs.**

### 2.2 Systems of record (SoR matrix)

| Concern | System of record | Role of the Grid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Certified enterprise meaning | Git `contracts/Semantic Control Plane/` | Authoritative concepts, maps, federation |
| Glossary prose & logical models | Collibra (Business Catalog) | Local ownership; maps *to* concepts |
| Physical / stream / API inventory | Collibra / Dataplex (Technical Catalog) | Assets *represent* concepts |
| Product identity, ports, ODCS | Entropy Marketplace (Data Products) | Products *implement* / *expose* concepts |
| Query & impact analysis | Neo4j (materialization) | Demo / runtime lens — **not** SoR |
| Package interchange | Apache OSSIE | Export/import packages — **not** a second SoR |

### 2.3 Design principles

1. **Do not fork global** — NATCO concepts federate to SID-seeded global concepts; they do not replace them.
2. **Crosswalks are first-class** — `mapsTo`, `represents`, and `implements` are versioned Mapping Records (DA-08 / DA-09 / DA-10), not soft “related to” tags.
3. **Convergence on URI** — Business, Technical, and Product layers must agree on the same Concept URI (e.g. `…/ns/global/customer`). Divergence is a governance event.
4. **Git as SoR, graph as lens** — Contracts version in Git; Neo4j answers Q1–Q7 / N1–N5 for humans and machines.
5. **Metric formulas stay beside the layer** — KPI *names* may `mapsTo` a Concept; calculation logic remains in BI / metric engines.

## 3. Four-pack architecture

All normative asset types live under [`contracts/`](../../contracts/). Machine relation inventory: [`contracts/cross-pack.relations.json`](../../contracts/cross-pack.relations.json).

### 3.1 Pack roles

```text
Business Catalog  ──mapsTo (DA-08)──────┐
Technical Catalog ──represents (DA-09)──┼──▶ Mapping Record ──targets──▶ Concept
Data Products     ──implements (DA-10)──┘         │
                                                  ▼
                                              Namespace
NATCO Concept ──Federation Edge (DA-11)──▶ global Concept
```

| Pack | Asset types (count) | Owns | Does not own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Semantic Control Plane** | 8 | Namespaces, concepts, ontologies, value sets, relations, mappings, federation, semantic policies | Catalog inventory, product lifecycle |
| **Business Catalog** | 16 | Terms, domains, entities, attributes, KPIs, policies, reports, … | Canonical concept definitions |
| **Technical Catalog** | 17 | Systems, DBs, schemas, tables, columns, topics, pipelines, APIs, … | Business meaning |
| **Data Products** | 9 | Products, ports, contracts, contract fields, teams, access, domains | Concept prose |

### 3.2 Semantic Control Plane assets

| Type | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Namespace | Scopes concept URIs (`global`, `natco-*`, option namespaces) |
| Concept | Unit of meaning (`entity`, `shared_property`, `metric`, …) |
| Ontology Package | Versioned publishable concept bundle |
| Value Set | Controlled values for properties |
| Concept Relation | Typed registry edge between concepts |
| Mapping Record | Crosswalk catalog/product → Concept |
| Federation Edge | NATCO / import Concept → `global` Concept |
| Semantic Policy | Rules for mapping, federation, approval |

### 3.3 Normative cross-pack bridges (X1–X19)

Selected predicates (full catalog in cross-pack docs):

| Predicate | From → To | DA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `mapsTo` | Business Term / Entity / Attribute / KPI → Concept | DA-08 |
| `represents` | Table / Column → Concept | DA-09 |
| `implements` / `exposes` | Data Product / Field → Concept | DA-10 |
| `federates` | NATCO Concept → global Concept | DA-11 |
| `implemented_in` / `implemented_by` | Entity↔Table, Attribute↔Column, Port↔Table, Field↔Column | structural |

**Convergence rule:** if business, technical, and product maps disagree on the Concept URI, freeze promotion, convene stewards, then federate, disambiguate, or defer with a `decision_ref` (SQ4).

## 4. Worked example: Customer 360 across NATCOs

### 4.1 Global concept hub

SID-aligned **Customer** in namespace `global` is the meaning hub. Impact analysis, Marketplace certification, and agent lookup start here — not at a table name.

### 4.2 NATCO federation

Each NATCO keeps a local concept and glossary term, linked by Mapping Records and Federation Edges:

| NATCO | Term | Local concept | Link to global |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DE | Kunde | `kunde` | `federates` / `mapsTo` → Customer |
| AT | Kunde | `kunde` | same pattern |
| HR | Kupac | `kupac` | same pattern |
| HU | Ügyfél | `ugyfel` | same pattern |
| PL | Klient | `klient` | same pattern |

### 4.3 Product families in the POC seed

Five Marketplace families × (1 global + 5 NATCOs) = **30 Data Products**, each with technical tables/columns, business entities/attributes, semantic concepts, and ODCS contract fields:

| Family | Completeness (cross-pack) |
| --- | --- |
| customer-360 | PASS |
| customer-interactions | PASS |
| product-orders | PASS |
| billing-accounts | PASS |
| service-subscriptions | PASS |

Approximate materialization scale (see [17. Cross-Pack Completeness](../kg/17.%20Cross-Pack%20Completeness.md)): ~30 products · ~30 tables · ~145 columns · ~151 contract fields · ~151 concepts.

### 4.4 End-to-end consumer path

1. Marketplace lists **Customer 360** (`dp-customer-360`).
2. Product **implements** `concept-global-Customer`.
3. Output port is **backed by** technical table(s); contract fields **implemented by** columns.
4. Columns **represent** property concepts (e.g. customer identifier); terms **mapTo** the same concepts.
5. NATCO stacks **federate** into global for group reporting and AI context.

Demo surfaces: `/demo/customer360/marketplace` (consumer), `/demo/customer360/contracts` (producer), `/demo/customer360/semantics` (KG).

## 6. Strategic stance (SQ1–SQ12)

Source of truth for the board: `frontend/src/data/strategic-questions.json` · hub: [16. Strategic Questions](../strategy/16.%20Strategic%20Questions.md).

### 6.1 Locked POC stance

1. Semantic layer = **Semantic Control Plane only**; metric calculations stay beside it.  
2. Meaning SoR = **Git Contracts (SCP)**; Marketplace owns products; Collibra owns glossary prose; Neo4j is not SoR.  
3. Consumers decide with **demos**, not slides — this repository is the demo harness.

### 6.2 Recommendations (summary)

| ID | Question | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SQ1 | What is the semantic layer? | Namespaces, certified concepts + URIs, MappingRecords, FederationEdges. KPI *names* may mapsTo; formulas stay in BI. |
| SQ2 | Where does the registry live? | Git SCP SoR. Marketplace may render meaning; does not own it. |
| SQ3 | People & machines? | Four journeys: Contracts UI, governance, Marketplace→Semantics, `/api/kg/*`. |
| SQ4 | Governance & conflict? | Happy path draft→review→approved. Conflict: freeze → convene → federate / disambiguate / defer + `decision_ref`. |
| SQ5 | Versioning? | Concept lifecycle states; ontology semver; mappings as versioned MappingRecords (most drift lives here). |
| SQ6 | Canonisation? | Promote when multi-domain/NATCO (or enterprise product), ~30-day stability, no open conflict, approval, ≥1 consumer. |
| SQ7 | Consumers? | Marketplace lineage, BI definition pickup, external API, agent-shaped queries. |
| SQ8 | Binding cost? | Inventory → AI first pass → steward confirm → MappingRecord + ODCS → measure hours. |
| SQ9 | Drift? | Concept-stage remapping vs MappingRecords; weekly pilot; tickets later. |
| SQ10 | Stewardship? | Named roles, reserved time, SLAs; capture hours before “ontology v1 for all NatCos”. |
| SQ11 | Ossie? | Interchange only. Round-trip protocol: export → second tool → checklist → Hold / gaps / Fail. |
| SQ12 | Strategy amendments? | Close semantic SoR row (Git SCP); pilot Customer + NATCOs; score-only SEM-V2 before enforce. |

Statuses remain **POC draft** / concept / protocol pending leadership gates — treat as programme recommendations evidenced by this Grid, not final corporate policy.

### 6.3 Semantic options (Germany · Power BI · Palantir)

| Option | Idea | Stance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Single Germany namespace; tools bind to it | Showcase (centralized) |
| **B** | Tool-owned namespaces | Retired / migration narrative only |
| **C** | BU namespaces (B2B / B2C / Network) as SoR; tools `MAPS_TO` BU; BU `FEDERATES` to global | **Recommended** |

Demo: `/demo/customer360/options`.

## 7. Differentiating from typical approaches

| Approach | What it solves | What the Grid adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise glossary | Shared definitions in one tool | Certified URIs + versioned crosswalks across packs |
| Technical catalog | “Where is the table?” | “What does this asset *represent*?” |
| Data Marketplace | Product discovery & access | Product **implements** certified meaning |
| Metric / semantic layers in BI | Calculation consistency | Enterprise concept SoR independent of one BI tool |
| Knowledge graph alone | Traversal | Git authority + contract packs + governance lifecycle |

Distinctive claims of this POC:

1. Meaning SoR ≠ catalog SoR ≠ product SoR.  
2. Explicit DA-coded predicates, not informal relatedness.  
3. Multi-NATCO federation as a first-class model (DE/AT/HR/HU/PL).  
4. Cross-pack completeness as a verifiable matrix, not a slide.  
5. Strategic decision board embedded in the same artefact as the demo.

## 8. Pilot path and residual risks

### 8.1 Recommended pilot

Approve a **Customer-domain** pilot:

- One global Registry + Namespace slice (SID Customer)  
- One NATCO federation set  
- Five Mapping Records (term, entity, table, column, product)  
- One Marketplace product bind  
- One Ossie package export round-trip  

Then expand families and NATCOs using the completeness checklist.

### 8.2 Residual risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Stewardship underfunded (SQ10) | Measure hours in pilot before scaling ontology programmes |
| Silent drift (SQ9) | Treat MappingRecords as the change surface; schedule remaps |
| Tool politics (Option A vs C) | Prefer Option C where BUs and tools already diverge |
| Confusing Ossie with SoR (SQ11) | Packages only; Git remains authority |
| Over-scoping “semantic layer” to include KPI math (SQ1) | Keep formulas beside the layer |

### 8.3 Objection handling (pitch)

- **“We already have Collibra.”** — Collibra stays SoR for glossary prose; the Grid is SoR for enterprise meaning and crosswalks.  
- **“Will NATCOs lose ownership?”** — No. Catalogs stay local; they federate to global SID concepts.  
- **“Is OSSIE our new repository?”** — No. Interchange packages only.  
- **“Too big.”** — Pilot one domain + one NATCO against global.

---

## 9. Conclusion

The Enterprise Governance Grid reframes semantic work as **enterprise infrastructure**: Git-backed meaning, federated NATCO ownership, Marketplace certification, and a queryable graph for people and machines. Catalogs and products remain essential — they are no longer asked to be the sole authority on what words mean for the group.

The POC in this repository demonstrates that architecture end-to-end: contract packs, Neo4j enrichment, kg-api, and a pitchable demo tenant aligned to SQ1–SQ12. The ask is not a big-bang ontology programme. It is a Customer-domain pilot that proves convergence on URI — then scales with evidence.
